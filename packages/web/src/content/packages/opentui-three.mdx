OpenTUI Three puts real-time 3D in a terminal application. A Three.js scene renders
headlessly on the GPU, and the result becomes terminal cells: a 3D viewport that sits in
a normal OpenTUI layout next to text and inputs, or fills the whole screen. By default
the scene renders at twice the cell resolution and downsamples, which keeps the output
sharp at terminal geometry.

Place a scene in the renderable tree with `ThreeRenderable`, or draw into a buffer you
manage with `ThreeCliRenderer`. Beyond rendering, the package carries what a small game
or visualization needs: texture loading, sprite sheets, instanced sprites, sprite
animation, particles and explosions, and adapters for the Rapier and Planck physics
engines. The 3D & Physics demos in [OpenTUI Examples](/packages/opentui-examples) show
all of it running.

The package is Bun-only: it drives the GPU through `bun-webgpu` and depends on `three`
0.177.0 directly. The [Three.js WebGPU reference](/docs/reference/three) documents the
API.
